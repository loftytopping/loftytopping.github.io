## PhD study

You can apply for an advertised project or propose your own idea that fits my research. Projects I
supervise usually combine a real environmental problem with modelling, machine learning, AI systems or
data infrastructure, and many are co-supervised with colleagues in computer science, engineering or health.

Common funding routes include:

- NERC Doctoral Training Partnerships
- EPSRC Centres for Doctoral Training, including the Aerosol & Droplet Science CDT
- ESRC studentships
- University of Manchester President's Doctoral Scholarships
- Industry or government co-funded studentships

Deadlines usually fall between January and March. Get in touch six to twelve months before you hope to
start, with a CV and a paragraph on what you would like to work on. The department's
[postgraduate research funding pages](https://www.ees.manchester.ac.uk/study/postgraduate-research/funding/)
list current scholarships.

## Postdoctoral positions and fellowships

Open positions are advertised through the University of Manchester jobs site and I will post them here
too. If nothing is open, I am still happy to talk about future opportunities and collaboration.

I welcome approaches from researchers who want to apply for a fellowship hosted at Manchester, for example:

- Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowships
- Royal Society Newton International Fellowships
- Leverhulme Trust Early Career Fellowships
- UKRI Future Leaders Fellowships
- Royal Society University Research Fellowships

Please contact me at least six months before the deadline so we have time to shape a strong proposal
and check the fit with my work.

## Visiting researchers

I host academic visitors for anything from a week to a year. Visitors typically get desk space and
computing access, use of library and computing resources, and the chance to join seminars and
collaborate with the people I work with. Most visitors arrange their own funding, though limited support
is occasionally available. Please get in touch at least four months ahead so we can sort out the
University's visitor arrangements. What is possible depends on space and funding at the time.

## Sabbaticals

Visiting faculty on sabbatical, usually for three to twelve months, are very welcome. As well as desk
space and research facilities, there are opportunities to co-supervise students, build joint projects
and give guest lectures by arrangement. Contact me six to twelve months in advance to plan a visit.

## Short research visits

Shorter exchanges of one to six months suit PhD students, early-career researchers and Masters students
on research placements. Typical visits involve a joint piece of research, training, data analysis or
modelling work, and taking part in seminars. Visitors normally bring their own funding, for example
from travel grants or exchange programmes. Please contact me three months in advance.

## Masters projects

For current University of Manchester Masters students, I offer dissertation projects in environmental
data science, modelling, machine learning and AI systems. Projects are allocated in January and February
and run over the summer, with regular supervision and access to computing resources. Check your
programme's internal project list, or email me with an idea of your own.

## Partnerships

If you represent a university or organisation interested in joint training, summer schools, exchanges or
